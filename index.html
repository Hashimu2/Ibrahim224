📋 Desk Review Presentation • Bayero University Kano

Desk Review: Malaria Incidence and Seasonal Trends Using Historical Data, Kaduna State, 2018–2022

Research Project by Ibrahim Hashimu • Note: Chapter 4 and 5 figures are simulated

Download Desk Review Slide (PPTX)

1. Problem

Managers lack local evidence on when malaria cases peak and whether the burden is rising. Kaduna’s routine DHIS2 records have not been analysed over several years, so planning relies on general assumptions about the season.

2. Literature Review

The concepts are sound: incidence, seasonality, limits of routine data. The empirical review is a placeholder: one WHO 2022 source (3.0M to 3.6M estimated cases, 2018 to 2021) repeated three times, and no peer-reviewed study.

3. Type of Research

Retrospective, quantitative, secondary-data time-series study. Unit: the month, 60 points from January 2018 to December 2022. Source: Kaduna DHIS2. Tools: Excel, trend and seasonal decomposition, optional SARIMA.

4. Solution

Analyse monthly counts to find the trend and seasonal peak, compute incidence per 1,000 where population data exist, and time nets, test kits, drugs and health education to the peak months. Example peak: August to October, 38.9% of cases.

✅ Strengths

Clear five-chapter structure, simulated data labelled, reported cases kept apart from incidence, ethics planned.

⚠️ Gaps Identified

No real data yet. No peer-reviewed literature. Text figures disagree with tables (Sep 432,000 vs 447,668). 2022 row missing. Chapters 4 and 5 duplicated.

⚖️ Desk Review Verdict

Sound framework, evidence still to come. Not ready as research until real DHIS2 records replace the worked example.

📊 31-Page Research Study (PDF)

Analysis of Malaria Incidence and Seasonal Trends Using Historical Data

Study Area: Kaduna State, Nigeria • Study Period: January 2018 – December 2022 • Author: Ibrahim Hashimu

Download Research PDF Open Full 31-Page PDF

Abstract

Malaria remains a major public health concern in Nigeria. This study examines the pattern of malaria cases and seasonal variation in Kaduna State, Nigeria, over a five-year period (January 2018 to December 2022) using a retrospective quantitative time-series approach. Using 60 monthly observations across five years, the study evaluates annual case patterns, monthly distributions, rainy versus dry season peaks (August to October), DHIS2 routine health information system data completeness, and evidence-based recommendations for timely commodity allocation (RDTs, antimalarial medicines, and ITNs) and vector-control planning.

Keywords: Malaria Incidence Seasonal Trend Historical Data Time-Series Analysis DHIS2 Public Health
